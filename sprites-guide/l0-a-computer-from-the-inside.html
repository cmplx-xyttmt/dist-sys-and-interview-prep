A computer from the inside

One rung per week: the reading in the Thursday 19:30 system-design slot, the mission the following Tuesday 19:30. Tick as you go; the checkpoint at the bottom is what marks the rung passed.

1 · After this rung you can say

  • what a process is, and what the kernel does that a process can't (talk to hardware, hand out memory, decide who runs)
  • what a filesystem is (names, directories, an index of which blocks hold which file) versus the block device under it (a disk seen as a numbered array of fixed-size blocks, typically 4 KB). The first reading below covers this
  • why a container is a normal process with a restricted view of the machine (the kernel features are called namespaces and cgroups; the second reading defines them) and a virtual machine is a whole second kernel with its own emulated hardware
  • what a container image is: a stack of tar archives plus a JSON manifest, in the format the Open Container Initiative (OCI) standardised; and why pulling and unpacking one is the slow part of starting a container
  • what a microVM is: a virtual machine with almost no emulated hardware, so it boots in milliseconds. Firecracker is the one Fly uses under every Sprite

Hint · If the pull is fast anyway

You are on a fast connection and the image is small. Pull something bigger, such as pytorch/pytorch (several GB), or read the pull's output: it lists the layers, and each is one of the tar archives from the say-list.
