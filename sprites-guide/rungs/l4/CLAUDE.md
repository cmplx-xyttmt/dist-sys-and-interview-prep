## After this rung he can say

- what leader-based replication is: one node accepts writes, the others apply a copy of its log
- the three things you can ship: SQL statements, the write-ahead log (the physical bytes of changed pages), or a logical change log (row-level changes), and what each is good and bad at
- what point-in-time restore means (rebuild the database as it was at a chosen moment) and why a log makes it possible
- what Litestream does: watches SQLite's write-ahead log and ships each transaction to object storage continuously (as raw WAL frames before version 0.5, as its own LTX transaction files since September 2025); and what it can't do: more than one writer, or zero lag

Every item above must come with an explanation or a named source by the time a step relies on it.
If he asks about one and the steps below haven't covered it yet, explain it yourself.

## Passing the rung

## The steps

### Step 0 · read · Reading 1: DDIA chapter 5, the leader-based replication half (up to the multi-leader section)

Read DDIA chapter 5, the leader-based replication half (up to the multi-leader section) (https://dataintensive.net/), core. 60 min. Replication logs, replication lag, and "read-your-writes" (a reader always sees its own writes even on a lagging replica).

Alongside: Draw the leader, two followers and one write as a sequence diagram before reading; correct it after.

Guess questions (ask before the reading or the prediction):
- If the leader dies, how does a follower know whether it has everything? What exactly is replication lag?
- Name a bug a user would see with a lagging replica.

### Step 1 · read · Reading 2: Litestream: how it works, second pass

Read Litestream: how it works, second pass (https://litestream.io/how-it-works/), core. 15 min. It now speaks of LTX files and TXIDs (transaction ids): the same WAL frames from rung L1, batched per transaction and compacted into levels.

Alongside: With `litestream replicate` running, list the bucket with the MinIO client every few seconds and watch files appear.

Guess questions (ask before the reading or the prediction):
- What is an LTX file compared with a WAL frame from rung L1? Why compact them?

### Step 2 · read · Reading 3: Litestream guide, S3-compatible storage

Read Litestream guide, S3-compatible storage (https://litestream.io/guides/s3-compatible/), core. 10 min, for the mission's exact configuration.

Guess questions (ask before the reading or the prediction):
- Why does path-style addressing matter for MinIO and not for real S3?

### Step 3 · do · Mission step 1

Install Litestream: `brew install benbjohnson/litestream/litestream` (check the install page (https://litestream.io/install/) if the tap has moved). Write `litestream.yml` with one database entry for `app.db` and one replica: `type: s3`, `bucket: ladder`, `path: app.db`, `endpoint: http://127.0.0.1:9000`, `force-path-style: true`, and the MinIO access and secret keys from L3.

### Step 4 · do · Mission step 2

Write `writer.py`: opens `app.db`, runs `PRAGMA journal_mode=WAL`, creates `rows(id integer primary key, t text)`, then inserts one row a second and prints `select count(*)` after each commit. Terminal 1: `litestream replicate -config litestream.yml`. Terminal 2: `python writer.py`.

### Step 5 · do · Mission step 3

After a minute, kill both. Delete `app.db`, `app.db-wal` and `app.db-shm` (the WAL file and its shared-memory index). Then `litestream restore -config litestream.yml -o restored.db app.db` and `sqlite3 restored.db 'select count(*) from rows'`.

### Step 6 · do · Mission step 4

Point-in-time: `litestream restore -config litestream.yml -timestamp 2026-...T..:..:..Z -o then.db app.db` with a timestamp 30 seconds before the kill; count rows again. The difference is the log replayed to a chosen moment.

### Step 7 · do · Mission step 5

Stretch: open `app.db-wal` with Python's `struct`, read the 32-byte header and the 24-byte frame headers, and print the page numbers. Compare with what rung L1's second reading described.
