Snapshots, copy-on-write, forking

One rung per week, in two sessions: the first half on Thursday at 19:30, the second half the following Tuesday at 19:30. Tick as you go; the checkpoint at the bottom is what marks the rung passed.

1 · After this rung you can say

  • what copy-on-write means: readers share one copy; a writer gets a private copy of only the part it changes
  • how an overlay filesystem works: a read-only lower directory, a writable upper directory holding only the changes, a work directory the kernel uses internally, and a merged view that shows both
  • why a virtual-machine snapshot can be taken in milliseconds (memory and disk are marked copy-on-write rather than copied) and what Firecracker's snapshot and restore do
  • why forking a disk from a snapshot is cheap, and why Sprites cannot yet do it natively: restore is in-place, and fork-from-checkpoint is a requested feature as of mid-2026. The explainer's section 05 describes forking as if it shipped; treat that paragraph as the plan, not the product

3 · Mission model it

Do this

The check: lower/ is byte-unchanged after all three edits (diff prints nothing); each upper holds only the one file changed through its own merged view; in chunks.py, the fork shares all but the one or two changed chunks with the original. Record the shared-out-of-total count. Then write two sentences on why Sprites can't fork from a checkpoint yet, from the community thread.

Hint · If mount says wrong fs type or invalid argument

Your upper or work directory is on the container's overlay root. Check df . shows tmpfs. If it says operation not permitted instead, you forgot --privileged. Either way the friction is the point: filesystems are a kernel matter, which is what rung L0 said.
